01/audit

What's already there, and what's worth keeping.

A review of techmids.org, conf.techmids.org and events.techmids.org. Three sites, three template defaults, one very good idea hiding in plain sight.

Keep

The yellow and the slant

  • #FFFF00 on black. Pure, loud, unmistakable. Nobody else in UK tech events owns it.
  • The italic wordmark, heavy TECH over light MIDS.
  • The yellow block with a tilted tag (Conf 2026, Events). It is already a system, it just hasn't been written down.
  • The warm, plain voice: "a friendly bunch", "the hallway track".
Fix

Three sites, three palettes

SiteAccentNeutral
techmids.org#EAB308zinc
conf#FFFF00gray + indigo glow
events#FFEE58slate

The main site has no logo at all, and uses a mustard that isn't the brand yellow. Blue-tinted greys fight the yellow and make it look green.

Add

Rules, tokens, a point of view

  • A warm neutral ramp so yellow stays yellow.
  • A real light mode. Yellow text on white fails; the guide says what to do instead.
  • A type system with a display face that has some character, plus mono for data.
  • One token file that both sites and the component library read from.

02/idea

Hi-vis for the people who build things.

The Midlands makes things. TechMids yellow is the yellow of a site jacket, a floor marking, a "mind the step" sign: practical, visible, slightly cheeky. That is the brand. It reads as built by engineers because it behaves like a well-kept codebase: few parts, clearly named, used consistently.

"Developery" here means precise and legible, not costume. No fake terminals, no green rain, no curly-brace puns, no circuit boards. The developer signal comes from three quiet habits:

  • Things are labelled. Small mono captions, like a spec sheet or a well-named variable.
  • Things have addresses. Sections are routes: /conf/2026/schedule.
  • The grid shows. Faint construction lines and corner ticks, like an inspector overlay left on.
Principle 1

Loud, then quiet

One yellow thing per view does the shouting. Everything else is ink, paper and good type.

Principle 2

Community first

Photos of real people at real events. The meetups' own logos get space. We are the frame, not the picture.

Principle 3

Plain and useful

Date, place, price, how to get in. Say it once, clearly, near the top.

Principle 4

Everyone gets in

AA contrast minimum, visible focus, both colour modes treated as first class.

Yellow is the 8%. If a screen is half yellow, it is a poster, and that's fine, once.

Rules
  • Yellow fills need an Ink edge on Paper or White: a 1px border, or Ink text sitting on top.
  • Emphasis in running text is a highlight, like this, not yellow type.
  • Yellow text is for links, dates and one headline phrase. Body copy stays off-white.
  • No pure white. #F2F1EC on Night is easier on the eyes than #FFF on #000.
  • Only Ink goes on yellow. Never white, grey or an accent.
  • No blue-tinted greys. Graphite only. No decorative gradients or glow blobs.
  • Accents carry meaning (status, track). Never use them as decoration.

07/type

Three typefaces, three jobs.

Display · headlines
Aa Gg

Bricolage Grotesque

A grotesque with a bit of a wonk: ink traps, uneven widths, real personality at large sizes. Replaces DM Sans. Weights 600 to 800, tracking -3%, line height 1.0 to 1.1.

All day, full stack, Brum.
Text · everything you read
Aa Gg

Inter

Already on the conf and events sites, so it stays. Body, UI, forms and long reads. Weights 400, 500, 600. Line height 1.6 for body, 1.25 for UI.

Hear from speakers from the local community and beyond, across three tracks.
Mono · data and labels
Aa 09

Martian Mono

Wide, sturdy, a little odd. Used small and uppercase for dates, times, prices, tags and labels. It is seasoning: never for paragraphs, never for headlines.

Fri 27 Nov · 09:00 · Track 2

Why not keep DM Sans? It is fine, and it is everywhere. Bricolage gives headlines a voice without touching the logo.

All three are open source (SIL OFL) and on Google Fonts as variable fonts. Self-host them with font-display: swap.

The slant belongs to the logo. Don't italicise headlines to match it. Italic is for titles of talks and for emphasis in body copy.

The Slant

9°, taken from the wordmark's stems. For dividers, progress, list bullets and section edges. Shapes slant; text doesn't.

The Tag

A mono label rotated 5°. Pins a name onto a photo or a block. One tag per image; it always tilts the same way.

12 col · 24 gutter
384 × 192

The Grid

Faint construction lines, corner ticks and dimension notes in hero areas and empty states. 1px, Graphite, never yellow.

Photography
  • Real events, real people, never stock.
  • Full colour, natural light where possible. No yellow duotone or filters.
  • Faces and hands: talking, laughing, building. Fewer empty rooms.
  • Always with alt text and the photographer's credit.

13/voice

Sound like the organiser who saved you a seat.

Friendly, not zany

Warm and a bit dry. "A friendly bunch" is the register. One joke per page, maximum.

Plain, not corporate

Short words, short sentences, British spelling. If you wouldn't say it at the pub after a meetup, rewrite it.

Specific, not hyped

Numbers, names and places beat adjectives. "250 seats, 3 tracks" beats "an incredible experience".

Open, not cliquey

Assume the reader is new. Explain in-jokes and acronyms. Say who it's for and what it costs.

Proudly local

Birmingham and the Midlands by name. "Brum" is welcome in headlines; forced dialect is not.

Say

Three tracks, 250 seats, one cinema. Friday 27 November.

Not

Join us for an unmissable, world-class celebration of innovation!

Say

Never given a talk before? Good. We'd love to hear from you.

Not

We are seeking submissions from thought leaders and industry experts.

Say

We're sold out. Join the waiting list and we'll email if a seat frees up.

Not

Error 418: tickets not found ¯\_(ツ)_/¯

Say

TechMids is run by volunteers and is not for profit. Ticket money pays for the venue, the food and the AV.

Not

Leveraging synergies across the regional tech ecosystem.

Naming

TechMids, one word, capital T and M. TechMids Conf 2026. Not "Tech Mids", "Techmids" or "TMC".

Dates and times

Fri 27 Nov 2026 · 09:00 in labels. "Friday 27 November" in prose. 24-hour clock.

Developer humour

Leave code jokes to the speakers. No <h1> tags in headlines, no "404: fun not found".
